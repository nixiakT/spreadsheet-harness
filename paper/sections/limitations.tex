\section{Limitations and conclusion}

\method{} checks configured evidence, not an instruction's full meaning.
Containment cannot show that a declaration matches user intent; compliance may
accompany a wrong answer, and fail-closed handling may reject a correct but
unsupported workbook. The exact-pair bound applies only to the defined raw
projection, not monitors that independently parse workbook bytes. Event
instrumentation can itself be wrong despite producer-diverse controls and an
independent oracle.

The trusted diff, adapters, predicates, recalculation backend, scorer, storage,
and auditor may misread macros, external links, arrays, embedded objects, or
vendor charts. LibreOffice/Excel disagreement is therefore measured separately.
Attempts are observations, not solutions; finalization checks observed
transitions and a fresh scan but does not replay every model witness. An audited
noncompletion has no accepted deliverable. Hash consistency also assumes a
trusted host: hostile rewriting needs signatures and an external transparency
anchor, not more local hashes.

Two model families, three seeds, service drift, and small suites limit
generalization. Publicly described tasks are excluded from the primary Debugging
stratum; the full 100-task estimate is sensitivity only. Visualization has no
object-grounding claim before typed-object audit; joint distillation is secondary.
We pair correctness with false completion, coverage, recovery, collateral damage,
cost, and backend disagreement. \method{} audits specified evidence and lineage,
not correctness or cryptographic security.
