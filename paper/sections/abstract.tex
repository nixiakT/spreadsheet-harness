\begin{abstract}
Spreadsheet agents can make plausible edits yet terminate after checking the
wrong range, reusing evidence from an older workbook state, or invalidating a
check during recalculation. Runtime policy engines can express artifact-aware
rules, but their guarantees depend on trusted domain events: ordinary tool-call
traces do not reveal what an opaque program changed inside a workbook. We
introduce \method, a spreadsheet observation and delivery layer that computes
semantic workbook effects, assigns monotonic artifact revisions, and supplies
policy engines with affected cells, formulas, structure, and rendered pages.
Prospective records bind a declared edit target to pre-edit observations before
the actual staged footprint is known; retrospective obligations require fresh
witnesses over that footprint. A submission is accepted only when harness-
emitted witnesses cover the required scope on the candidate revision. For an
accepted candidate, postprocessing emits a content-addressed consistency
certificate for a byte-identical read-only scoring replica. The finalizer
explicitly distinguishes that accepted deliverable from an audited
noncompletion: the latter receives only an observer-generated finalization
integrity record for its termination and artifact lineage, without claiming a
successful submission, accepted deliverable, or task correctness. We evaluate
the approach with controlled contract ablations,
an independently implemented adversarial OOXML oracle, SpreadsheetBench, and an
instance-artifact-unseen 100-task SpreadsheetBench~2 debugging study.
